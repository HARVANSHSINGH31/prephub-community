# Contributing a Guide

Thank you for giving back to your juniors. This should take about 15–20 minutes.

## 1. Get verified (one-time)

Open an issue titled `Verify: <your name>` with:
- Your name, graduation batch, and current company/role
- A link to your LinkedIn profile
- Your college email (if you still have access) OR a way for the placement cell to confirm you graduated from this campus

Once a maintainer or the placement cell confirms it, you're added to `VERIFIED_CONTRIBUTORS.md` and can submit guides under your verified name.

## 2. Write your guide

- Copy `companies/TEMPLATE.md` into `companies/<company-name>/<role>.md`
- Fill in every section — skills required, round-by-round breakdown, question *types* you saw, and where you'd point someone to prepare
- Set `last_updated` and `still_accurate_as_of` to today's date

## 3. What to share — and what not to

- **Share:** the round structure, topics tested, difficulty level, how you prepared, what you'd do differently, general question *categories* (e.g. "graph traversal, one medium DP problem, a system design round on a URL shortener")
- **Don't share:** verbatim proprietary questions if your offer letter or company policy has an NDA/confidentiality clause covering interview content. When in doubt, describe the *type* of question, not the exact one word-for-word. This keeps the guide safe for you and for the project.

## 4. Submit

Open a pull request. A maintainer or placement-cell reviewer will merge it — usually within a couple of days.

## 5. Keep it alive

Once a year (or whenever the company changes its process), you'll get a reminder to confirm your guide is still accurate. A 30-second "yep, still accurate" PR keeps your guide trusted instead of stale. This one habit is what killed similar projects in the past — don't let it happen here.

## Answering student questions

If a student tags you in a **Discussions → Ask an Alum** thread, a reply whenever you have five minutes is genuinely valuable — and it's public, so it only has to be answered once per question, ever.
