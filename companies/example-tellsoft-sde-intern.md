## Required skill set

- DSA: arrays, strings, hashmaps, basic graph traversal (BFS/DFS) — this is 70% of the bar
- One core CS subject shows up in every loop: usually OS or DBMS basics
- Communication matters more than people think — they reject strong coders who can't explain their approach out loud

## Interview process (round by round)

1. **Online Assessment** — 90 minutes, 2 DSA problems (easy–medium) + 15 MCQs on CS fundamentals
2. **Technical Round 1** — 45 minutes, one medium DSA problem solved live, expect follow-up questions on time/space complexity
3. **Technical Round 2** — 45 minutes, one CS-fundamentals deep dive (they picked DBMS for me) + a short design question ("how would you design a URL shortener")
4. **HR / Culture Round** — 20 minutes, mostly about internships/projects on your resume — know your own projects cold

## How I prepared

- Did about 150 DSA problems over 10 weeks, focused on arrays/strings/graphs first
- Revised OS + DBMS basics from a single condensed course rather than a full textbook
- Did 3 mock interviews with seniors before the real thing — this mattered more than more DSA grinding would have

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Jumping to code before stating the approach out loud — interviewers dock heavily for this
- Ignoring edge cases until prompted — mention them yourself before they ask
